Indian Geography

Q1. The Standard Meridian of India passes through which longitude?

  1. 82°30′ E
  2. 77° E
  3. 90° E
  4. 0° (Prime Meridian)
Show answer

A. 82°30′ E — India's Standard Meridian is 82°30′ E, passing near Mirzapur (Uttar Pradesh); Indian Standard Time is based on it, 5 hours 30 minutes ahead of GMT.

Indian Geography

Q2. The longest river in India is the:

  1. Godavari
  2. Ganga
  3. Brahmaputra
  4. Yamuna
Show answer

B. Ganga — The Ganga is the longest river within India (about 2,525 km). The Godavari is the longest river of peninsular (South) India, sometimes called the 'Dakshina Ganga'.

Indian Geography

Q3. The largest state of India by area is:

  1. Madhya Pradesh
  2. Maharashtra
  3. Rajasthan
  4. Uttar Pradesh
Show answer

C. Rajasthan — Rajasthan is the largest Indian state by area. Madhya Pradesh is second; Uttar Pradesh is the most populous state, not the largest by area.

Indian Geography

Q4. The southernmost tip of the Indian mainland is:

  1. Indira Point
  2. Kanyakumari
  3. Rameswaram
  4. Point Calimere
Show answer

B. Kanyakumari — Kanyakumari (Cape Comorin) is the southernmost tip of the Indian mainland. Indira Point, in the Andaman & Nicobar Islands, is the southernmost point of the whole of India's territory.

Physical Geography

Q5. The Himalayas are an example of which type of mountains?

  1. Block mountains
  2. Volcanic mountains
  3. Fold mountains
  4. Residual mountains
Show answer

C. Fold mountains — The Himalayas are young fold mountains, formed by the collision of the Indian and Eurasian plates. Block mountains form by faulting; volcanic mountains from lava; residual mountains by erosion of older highlands.

World Geography

Q6. The largest ocean on Earth is the:

  1. Atlantic Ocean
  2. Indian Ocean
  3. Arctic Ocean
  4. Pacific Ocean
Show answer

D. Pacific Ocean — The Pacific Ocean is the largest and deepest ocean, covering about a third of the Earth's surface. The Atlantic is the second largest.

Indian Geography Location

Q7. The Tropic of Cancer passes through how many Indian states?

  1. Six
  2. Seven
  3. Eight
  4. Nine
Show answer

C. Eight — The Tropic of Cancer (23.5°N) crosses eight states: Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura and Mizoram.

Indian Soils

Q8. Which soil type, formed from weathered basaltic lava of the Deccan, is considered most suitable for cotton cultivation?

  1. Alluvial soil
  2. Red soil
  3. Black soil
  4. Laterite soil
Show answer

C. Black soil — Black soil (regur), rich in clay and highly moisture-retentive, developed over the Deccan Trap basalt and is ideal for cotton, hence its name 'black cotton soil'.

Indian Physiography

Q9. The Western Ghats and the Eastern Ghats meet at which range of hills?

  1. Anaimalai Hills
  2. Nilgiri Hills
  3. Cardamom Hills
  4. Palni Hills
Show answer

B. Nilgiri Hills — The Western and Eastern Ghats converge at the Nilgiri Hills in the south, where Doddabetta is the highest peak of the Nilgiris.

Indian Climate Local Winds

Q10. The hot, dry, dust-laden wind that blows over the northern plains of India during the afternoons of summer is called the:

  1. Kalbaisakhi
  2. Loo
  3. Mango shower
  4. Norwester
Show answer

B. Loo — The 'Loo' is a strong, hot and dry wind that blows over the north Indian plains from the west in May and June and can cause heat strokes.

Oceanography Currents

Q11. Which of the following is a cold ocean current?

  1. Gulf Stream
  2. Kuroshio Current
  3. Labrador Current
  4. Brazil Current
Show answer

C. Labrador Current — The Labrador Current flows southward from the Arctic along the coast of Canada, carrying cold water; the Gulf Stream, Kuroshio and Brazil currents are all warm currents.

Indian Physiography

Q12. Anamudi, the highest peak of peninsular India, is located in which range?

  1. Nilgiri Hills
  2. Aravalli Range
  3. Western Ghats
  4. Satpura Range
Show answer

C. Western Ghats — Anamudi (about 2,695 m) lies in the Western Ghats in Kerala and is the highest peak in India south of the Himalayas.

Climatology Earth Motion

Q13. In the Northern Hemisphere, the Coriolis force deflects moving winds and currents towards their:

  1. Left
  2. Right
  3. Centre
  4. Point of origin
Show answer

B. Right — Due to the Earth's rotation, the Coriolis force deflects freely moving objects to the right in the Northern Hemisphere and to the left in the Southern Hemisphere (Ferrel's law).

Indian Lakes

Q14. Which of the following is the largest inland saltwater (saline) lake in India?

  1. Chilika Lake
  2. Wular Lake
  3. Vembanad Lake
  4. Sambhar Lake
Show answer

D. Sambhar Lake — Sambhar Lake in Rajasthan is India's largest inland saline lake. Wular is the largest freshwater lake, Chilika the largest brackish lagoon, and Vembanad the longest lake.

World Physical Geography

Q15. Which water body separates India from Sri Lanka?

  1. Palk Strait
  2. Bering Strait
  3. Strait of Malacca
  4. Strait of Hormuz
Show answer

A. Palk Strait — The Palk Strait (with the Gulf of Mannar to its south) separates the Indian mainland from the island of Sri Lanka.

Climatology Pressure Belts

Q16. The belt of low pressure near the equator, where the trade winds converge and winds are generally calm, is known as the:

  1. Horse latitudes
  2. Doldrums
  3. Roaring forties
  4. Polar high
Show answer

B. Doldrums — The equatorial low-pressure belt is called the doldrums; intense heating causes rising air and calm surface conditions where the trade winds meet at the ITCZ.

Indian Rivers

Q17. The Godavari, the largest river of peninsular India, originates near which place?

  1. Amarkantak
  2. Mahabaleshwar
  3. Trimbakeshwar
  4. Yamunotri
Show answer

C. Trimbakeshwar — The Godavari rises near Trimbakeshwar in the Western Ghats of Maharashtra. Amarkantak is the source of the Narmada and Son, and Mahabaleshwar of the Krishna.

Climatology Precipitation

Q18. Rainfall caused when moisture-laden winds are forced to rise over a mountain barrier is classified as:

  1. Convectional rainfall
  2. Orographic rainfall
  3. Cyclonic rainfall
  4. Frontal rainfall
Show answer

B. Orographic rainfall — Orographic (relief) rainfall occurs when air rising over mountains cools, condenses and precipitates on the windward side, leaving a rain shadow on the leeward side.

Oceanography Relief

Q19. The deepest known point in the world's oceans, the Challenger Deep, lies in which trench?

  1. Puerto Rico Trench
  2. Mariana Trench
  3. Sunda Trench
  4. Tonga Trench
Show answer

B. Mariana Trench — The Mariana Trench in the western Pacific contains the Challenger Deep, the deepest point of the world's oceans at roughly 11,000 metres.

World Political Geography

Q20. Which continent contains the largest number of countries?

  1. Asia
  2. Europe
  3. Africa
  4. South America
Show answer

C. Africa — Africa has 54 sovereign countries, the most of any continent, more than Asia's 48 or Europe's roughly 44.

World Geography Time

Q21. The International Date Line approximately follows which meridian of longitude?

  1. 0°
  2. 90° East
  3. 180°
  4. 82°30' East
Show answer

C. 180° — The International Date Line runs approximately along the 180° meridian (with deviations to avoid landmasses), where the calendar date changes by one day.

Indian Rivers

Q22. Majuli, the largest river island in the world, is situated in which river?

  1. Brahmaputra
  2. Ganga
  3. Godavari
  4. Mahanadi
Show answer

A. Brahmaputra — Majuli, in Assam, is the world's largest river island and lies in the Brahmaputra river; it is also a notable centre of Assamese Vaishnavite culture.
