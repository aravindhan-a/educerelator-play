Freedom Struggle

Q1. The Quit India Movement was launched in the year:

  1. 1930
  2. 1942
  3. 1919
  4. 1947
Show answer

B. 1942 — The Quit India Movement was launched by Mahatma Gandhi on 8 August 1942 at the Bombay session of the Congress. 1930 was the Civil Disobedience/Salt Movement and 1919 the Rowlatt/Jallianwala Bagh events.

Modern History

Q2. Who was the first Governor-General of independent India?

  1. C. Rajagopalachari
  2. Lord Mountbatten
  3. Lord Canning
  4. Dr. Rajendra Prasad
Show answer

B. Lord Mountbatten — Lord Mountbatten was the first Governor-General of independent India (1947–48). C. Rajagopalachari was the first and only Indian to hold the post, who succeeded him.

Freedom Struggle

Q3. The Indian National Congress was founded in the year:

  1. 1857
  2. 1885
  3. 1905
  4. 1919
Show answer

B. 1885 — The Indian National Congress was founded in 1885, with A. O. Hume playing a key role and W. C. Bonnerjee as its first president. 1857 was the Revolt; 1905 the Partition of Bengal.

Modern History

Q4. The Battle of Plassey, which paved the way for British rule in Bengal, was fought in:

  1. 1757
  2. 1764
  3. 1857
  4. 1707
Show answer

A. 1757 — The Battle of Plassey (1757) was won by Robert Clive against Siraj-ud-Daulah. The Battle of Buxar followed in 1764; the Revolt of 1857 came a century later.

Freedom Struggle

Q5. Who gave the famous call 'Do or Die' during the Quit India Movement?

  1. Subhas Chandra Bose
  2. Jawaharlal Nehru
  3. Mahatma Gandhi
  4. Bhagat Singh
Show answer

C. Mahatma Gandhi — Mahatma Gandhi gave the 'Do or Die' call in his Quit India speech of August 1942. (Subhas Chandra Bose's slogan was 'Give me blood and I will give you freedom'.)

Ancient History

Q6. The Indus Valley (Harappan) Civilisation is especially noted for its:

  1. Iron weapons
  2. Planned towns and drainage
  3. Cave paintings
  4. Coinage of gold
Show answer

B. Planned towns and drainage — The Harappan Civilisation is famous for its well-planned towns, grid street layout, and advanced drainage/sanitation. It was a Bronze Age culture (iron was not yet in use).

Ancient Art Architecture

Q7. The Gandhara school of art, known for its Greco-Roman influence on Buddhist sculpture, flourished primarily under the patronage of which dynasty?

  1. The Kushanas
  2. The Guptas
  3. The Mauryas
  4. The Satavahanas
Show answer

A. The Kushanas — The Gandhara school reached its peak under the Kushana rulers, especially Kanishka, blending Hellenistic techniques with Buddhist themes.

Religious Movements

Q8. Which of the following was NOT one of the four noble truths preached by Gautama Buddha?

  1. The world is full of suffering
  2. Desire is the cause of suffering
  3. Suffering can be ended
  4. Rituals and sacrifices lead to salvation
Show answer

D. Rituals and sacrifices lead to salvation — Buddha rejected elaborate rituals and sacrifices. The Four Noble Truths concern suffering, its cause (desire), its cessation, and the path to end it.

Medieval India

Q9. The Vijayanagara Empire was founded by Harihara and Bukka, who are associated with which dynasty?

  1. Sangama dynasty
  2. Saluva dynasty
  3. Tuluva dynasty
  4. Aravidu dynasty
Show answer

A. Sangama dynasty — Harihara I and Bukka I founded Vijayanagara in 1336 and belonged to the Sangama dynasty, the first of the four dynasties that ruled the empire.

British Expansion

Q10. The doctrine of 'Lapse', used to annex Indian states, is most closely associated with which Governor-General?

  1. Lord Dalhousie
  2. Lord Wellesley
  3. Lord Cornwallis
  4. Lord William Bentinck
Show answer

A. Lord Dalhousie — Lord Dalhousie applied the Doctrine of Lapse to annex states like Satara, Jhansi and Nagpur when a ruler died without a natural heir.

Mughal Empire

Q11. Which Mughal emperor abolished the pilgrimage tax and the jizya, promoting a policy of religious tolerance?

  1. Akbar
  2. Babur
  3. Aurangzeb
  4. Shah Jahan
Show answer

A. Akbar — Akbar abolished the jizya (tax on non-Muslims) and the pilgrimage tax as part of his policy of sulh-i-kul (universal peace).

Colonial Economy

Q12. The Ryotwari system of land revenue, under which settlement was made directly with the cultivator, was introduced primarily in which region?

  1. Madras and Bombay Presidencies
  2. Bengal and Bihar
  3. The Gangetic plains of Awadh
  4. Punjab and the North-West
Show answer

A. Madras and Bombay Presidencies — The Ryotwari system, developed by Thomas Munro and Alexander Read, was introduced mainly in the Madras and Bombay Presidencies, making the ryot (cultivator) the revenue payer.

Socio Religious Reform

Q13. The 'Arya Samaj' was founded in 1875 by which social reformer?

  1. Swami Dayananda Saraswati
  2. Raja Ram Mohan Roy
  3. Swami Vivekananda
  4. Keshab Chandra Sen
Show answer

A. Swami Dayananda Saraswati — Swami Dayananda Saraswati founded the Arya Samaj in 1875, advocating a return to the Vedas and opposing idolatry and caste rigidity.

Mauryan Empire

Q14. Which edict-issuing ancient Indian ruler adopted 'Dhamma' as a code of moral conduct after the Kalinga war?

  1. Ashoka
  2. Chandragupta Maurya
  3. Bindusara
  4. Samudragupta
Show answer

A. Ashoka — Ashoka embraced Buddhism and propagated Dhamma through his rock and pillar edicts after being moved by the bloodshed of the Kalinga war.

Revolutionary Nationalism

Q15. The Ghadar Party, formed to overthrow British rule through armed revolt, was established in 1913 in which country?

  1. United States
  2. Germany
  3. Japan
  4. Afghanistan
Show answer

A. United States — The Ghadar Party was founded in San Francisco, USA, in 1913 by Indian immigrants including Lala Har Dayal and Sohan Singh Bhakna.

Early Nationalism

Q16. Who among the following presided over the first session of the Indian National Congress in 1885?

  1. W. C. Bonnerjee
  2. A. O. Hume
  3. Dadabhai Naoroji
  4. Surendranath Banerjee
Show answer

A. W. C. Bonnerjee — Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee was the president of the first Congress session held in Bombay in 1885; A. O. Hume was the general secretary.

National Movement

Q17. The Rowlatt Act of 1919, which the British passed despite Indian opposition, is best described as a law that allowed:

  1. Detention without trial
  2. A ban on the Indian National Congress
  3. An increase in the salt tax
  4. Separate electorates for Muslims
Show answer

A. Detention without trial — The Rowlatt Act empowered the government to imprison people without trial, provoking nationwide protest and leading to the Jallianwala Bagh tragedy.

Ancient Metallurgy

Q18. The famous Iron Pillar at Mehrauli, noted for its rust-resistant composition, dates to which period?

  1. Gupta period
  2. Maurya period
  3. Kushana period
  4. Chola period
Show answer

A. Gupta period — The Mehrauli Iron Pillar, bearing an inscription referring to a king 'Chandra', is generally attributed to the Gupta period and reflects advanced metallurgy.

Medieval Religion

Q19. Which Sikh Guru compiled the Adi Granth, the original version of the Sikh holy scripture?

  1. Guru Arjan Dev
  2. Guru Nanak
  3. Guru Gobind Singh
  4. Guru Tegh Bahadur
Show answer

A. Guru Arjan Dev — Guru Arjan Dev, the fifth Sikh Guru, compiled the Adi Granth in 1604 at Amritsar, incorporating hymns of earlier Gurus and various saints.

Colonial Land Revenue

Q20. The 'Permanent Settlement' of 1793, which fixed land revenue in perpetuity, was introduced by which official?

  1. Lord Cornwallis
  2. Warren Hastings
  3. Lord Ripon
  4. Robert Clive
Show answer

A. Lord Cornwallis — Lord Cornwallis introduced the Permanent Settlement (Zamindari system) in Bengal in 1793, fixing revenue payable by zamindars permanently.

Ancient Buddhism

Q21. Kanishka, one of the greatest Kushana rulers, is credited with convening the Fourth Buddhist Council, which was held in:

  1. Kashmir
  2. Rajgir
  3. Vaishali
  4. Pataliputra
Show answer

A. Kashmir — The Fourth Buddhist Council was held in Kashmir under Kanishka's patronage, and it is associated with the formal rise of Mahayana Buddhism.

National Movement

Q22. The 'Poona Pact' of 1932 was an agreement between Mahatma Gandhi and which leader concerning representation of the depressed classes?

  1. B. R. Ambedkar
  2. Jawaharlal Nehru
  3. Muhammad Ali Jinnah
  4. Vallabhbhai Patel
Show answer

A. B. R. Ambedkar — The Poona Pact was signed between Gandhi and B. R. Ambedkar, replacing separate electorates for the depressed classes with reserved seats within the general electorate.
