Solutions

Q1. The number of moles of solute dissolved per litre of solution is called:

  1. Molality
  2. Molarity
  3. Normality
  4. Mole fraction
Show answer

B. Molarity — Molarity (M) = moles of solute per litre of solution. Molality is moles of solute per kilogram of solvent.

Organic Chemistry

Q2. The functional group present in carboxylic acids is:

  1. –OH
  2. –CHO
  3. –COOH
  4. –NH₂
Show answer

C. –COOH — Carboxylic acids contain the carboxyl group –COOH. –OH is alcohol, –CHO is aldehyde, and –NH₂ is amine.

Electrochemistry

Q3. The process of coating iron with a layer of zinc to protect it from rusting is called:

  1. Galvanisation
  2. Vulcanisation
  3. Electrolysis
  4. Annealing
Show answer

A. Galvanisation — Galvanisation is coating iron/steel with zinc; the zinc corrodes preferentially (sacrificial protection), shielding the iron. Vulcanisation is a process for rubber.

Equilibrium

Q4. The pH of a 0.001 M (10⁻³ M) HCl solution is:

  1. 1
  2. 2
  3. 3
  4. 11
Show answer

C. 3 — HCl is a strong acid that fully ionises, so [H⁺] = 10⁻³ M. pH = −log[H⁺] = −log(10⁻³) = 3.

Equilibrium

Q5. Which of the following is a strong acid?

  1. Acetic acid
  2. Carbonic acid
  3. Hydrochloric acid
  4. Citric acid
Show answer

C. Hydrochloric acid — Hydrochloric acid (HCl) ionises completely in water and is a strong acid. Acetic, carbonic and citric acids ionise only partially and are weak acids.

Redox

Q6. The oxidation state of oxygen in most of its compounds is:

  1. +2
  2. −2
  3. −1
  4. 0
Show answer

B. −2 — Oxygen usually has an oxidation state of −2 (as in H₂O, CO₂). Exceptions include peroxides (−1, as in H₂O₂) and compounds with fluorine.

Solutions

Q7. Which of the following is a colligative property of a dilute solution?

  1. Boiling point of the pure solvent
  2. Depression in freezing point
  3. Molar mass of the solute
  4. Density of the solution
Show answer

B. Depression in freezing point — Colligative properties depend only on the number of solute particles, not their nature. Depression in freezing point is one such property (along with relative lowering of vapour pressure, elevation of boiling point, and osmotic pressure).

Solutions

Q8. Assuming complete dissociation, the van't Hoff factor (i) for a dilute aqueous solution of K2SO4 is:

  1. 1
  2. 2
  3. 3
  4. 4
Show answer

C. 3 — K2SO4 dissociates into 2 K+ ions and 1 SO4^2- ion, giving 3 particles per formula unit, so i = 3 for complete dissociation.

Electrochemistry

Q9. When a strong electrolyte solution is progressively diluted, its molar conductivity:

  1. Decreases sharply
  2. Increases slightly and approaches a limiting value
  3. Remains exactly constant
  4. Falls to zero
Show answer

B. Increases slightly and approaches a limiting value — For a strong electrolyte the number of ions is nearly constant; on dilution inter-ionic attractions decrease, so molar conductivity rises slightly and approaches the limiting molar conductivity (Λ°m).

Chemical Kinetics

Q10. For a first-order reaction, which statement about its half-life is correct?

  1. It decreases steadily with time
  2. It doubles when the initial concentration doubles
  3. It is independent of the initial concentration
  4. It is numerically equal to the rate constant
Show answer

C. It is independent of the initial concentration — For a first-order reaction t½ = 0.693/k, which contains no concentration term, so the half-life is independent of the initial concentration of the reactant.

Chemical Kinetics

Q11. The units of the rate constant for a zero-order reaction are:

  1. s^-1
  2. mol L^-1 s^-1
  3. L mol^-1 s^-1
  4. mol^2 L^-2 s^-1
Show answer

B. mol L^-1 s^-1 — For a zero-order reaction, rate = k (rate has units mol L^-1 s^-1 and concentration term is raised to power zero), so k also has units of mol L^-1 s^-1.

D And F Block

Q12. The characteristic colour of most transition metal ions in their compounds is chiefly due to:

  1. d-d electronic transitions
  2. Presence of unpaired s-electrons
  3. Nuclear transitions
  4. A completely filled d subshell
Show answer

A. d-d electronic transitions — In the presence of ligands the d-orbitals split; electrons absorb visible light to move between these split d-levels (d-d transitions), and the complementary colour is transmitted, giving the ion its colour.

D And F Block

Q13. Lanthanoid contraction is best described as:

  1. An increase in atomic size down the group
  2. A sudden expansion of the 4f orbitals
  3. A loss of magnetic character across the series
  4. A steady decrease in atomic and ionic radii across the lanthanoid series
Show answer

D. A steady decrease in atomic and ionic radii across the lanthanoid series — As the atomic number increases across the lanthanoids, the added 4f electrons shield the nuclear charge poorly, so the effective nuclear charge felt by outer electrons rises and atomic/ionic radii decrease steadily.

Coordination Compounds

Q14. In the coordination compound [Co(NH3)6]Cl3, the oxidation state of cobalt is:

  1. +2
  2. +3
  3. +1
  4. +6
Show answer

B. +3 — NH3 is neutral, so the +3 charge balancing the three Cl- ions resides entirely on cobalt; hence Co is in the +3 oxidation state.

Coordination Compounds

Q15. Which of the following can act as an ambidentate ligand?

  1. Cl-
  2. NH3
  3. SCN-
  4. H2O
Show answer

C. SCN- — An ambidentate ligand has two different donor atoms but binds through only one at a time. SCN- can coordinate through sulphur (thiocyanato) or nitrogen (isothiocyanato).

Haloalkanes

Q16. Which alkyl halide is most reactive towards an SN1 (unimolecular) substitution reaction?

  1. Chloromethane
  2. 1-chlorobutane
  3. 2-chlorobutane
  4. tert-butyl chloride
Show answer

D. tert-butyl chloride — SN1 rate depends on the stability of the carbocation intermediate. tert-butyl chloride forms a tertiary carbocation, the most stable of the options, so it reacts fastest by SN1.

Alcohols Phenols Ethers

Q17. Among the following, the compound with the greatest acidic character is:

  1. Ethanol
  2. Phenol
  3. Cyclohexanol
  4. Methanol
Show answer

B. Phenol — Phenol's conjugate base (phenoxide) is stabilised by resonance delocalisation of the negative charge into the ring, whereas the alkyl alcohols form non-resonance-stabilised alkoxides; hence phenol is far more acidic.

Aldehydes Ketones

Q18. Which of the following compounds gives a positive iodoform test?

  1. Benzaldehyde
  2. Formaldehyde
  3. Acetone
  4. Diethyl ether
Show answer

C. Acetone — The iodoform test is positive for compounds containing the CH3CO- (methyl ketone) group or those oxidisable to it. Acetone (CH3COCH3) has this group; benzaldehyde and formaldehyde lack a CH3CO- group.

Aldehydes Ketones

Q19. Which aldehyde undergoes the Cannizzaro reaction on treatment with concentrated alkali?

  1. Acetaldehyde
  2. Propanal
  3. Butanal
  4. Benzaldehyde
Show answer

D. Benzaldehyde — The Cannizzaro reaction occurs only with aldehydes that have no alpha-hydrogen. Benzaldehyde has no alpha-H, so it disproportionates into benzyl alcohol and benzoate; the others all possess alpha-hydrogens.

Amines

Q20. The carbylamine reaction (formation of a foul-smelling isocyanide with CHCl3 and alcoholic KOH) is a laboratory test for:

  1. Tertiary amines
  2. Secondary amines
  3. Primary amines
  4. Quaternary ammonium salts
Show answer

C. Primary amines — Only primary amines (aliphatic or aromatic) react with chloroform and alcoholic KOH to give isocyanides (carbylamines) with an offensive smell; secondary and tertiary amines do not respond.

Biomolecules

Q21. Which of the following carbohydrates is a disaccharide?

  1. Glucose
  2. Fructose
  3. Sucrose
  4. Starch
Show answer

C. Sucrose — Sucrose on hydrolysis yields one molecule each of glucose and fructose, so it is a disaccharide. Glucose and fructose are monosaccharides and starch is a polysaccharide.

Biomolecules

Q22. The covalent linkage that joins successive amino acid residues in a protein chain is the:

  1. Glycosidic bond
  2. Phosphodiester bond
  3. Hydrogen bond
  4. Peptide bond
Show answer

D. Peptide bond — Amino acids are linked by peptide (amide, -CO-NH-) bonds formed by condensation between the carboxyl group of one amino acid and the amino group of the next; glycosidic and phosphodiester bonds occur in carbohydrates and nucleic acids respectively.
