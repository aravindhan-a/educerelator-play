Units Measurement

Q1. The SI unit of force is the:

  1. Joule
  2. Newton
  3. Watt
  4. Pascal
Show answer

B. Newton — Force is measured in newtons (N), where 1 N = 1 kg·m/s². The joule is the unit of energy, the watt of power, and the pascal of pressure.

Mechanics

Q2. The acceleration due to gravity near the Earth's surface is approximately:

  1. 9.8 m/s²
  2. 98 m/s²
  3. 0.98 m/s²
  4. 3 × 10⁸ m/s²
Show answer

A. 9.8 m/s² — Near the Earth's surface, g ≈ 9.8 m/s² (often taken as 9.81 or 10 for estimates). 3 × 10⁸ m/s is the speed of light, not an acceleration.

Electricity

Q3. The SI unit of electric current is the:

  1. Volt
  2. Ohm
  3. Ampere
  4. Coulomb
Show answer

C. Ampere — Electric current is measured in amperes (A). The volt is the unit of potential difference, the ohm of resistance, and the coulomb of electric charge.

Optics

Q4. The splitting of white light into its constituent colours by a prism is called:

  1. Reflection
  2. Diffraction
  3. Dispersion
  4. Polarisation
Show answer

C. Dispersion — Dispersion is the separation of white light into colours because different wavelengths refract by different amounts. Reflection is bouncing back; diffraction is bending around obstacles; polarisation restricts the vibration direction.

Modern Physics

Q5. The speed of light in vacuum is approximately:

  1. 3 × 10⁶ m/s
  2. 3 × 10⁸ m/s
  3. 3 × 10¹⁰ m/s
  4. 3 × 10⁵ m/s
Show answer

B. 3 × 10⁸ m/s — Light travels at about 3 × 10⁸ m/s (299,792,458 m/s) in vacuum. This is a universal constant, denoted c.

Units Measurement

Q6. The SI unit of work (and energy) is the:

  1. Newton
  2. Watt
  3. Joule
  4. Newton-second
Show answer

C. Joule — Work and energy share the unit joule (J), where 1 J = 1 N·m. The newton is force, the watt is power (J/s), and the newton-second is impulse/momentum.

Kinematics

Q7. A ball is thrown vertically upward with speed 20 m/s from the ground (take g = 10 m/s²). Neglecting air resistance, what is the total time it stays in the air before returning to the ground?

  1. 2 s
  2. 4 s
  3. 6 s
  4. 1 s
Show answer

B. 4 s — Time to rise = u/g = 20/10 = 2 s. By symmetry the fall takes an equal 2 s, so total time of flight = 4 s.

Kinematics

Q8. A body moves in a straight line and its velocity–time graph is a straight line passing through the origin with positive slope. Which quantity is constant for this motion?

  1. Velocity
  2. Displacement
  3. Acceleration
  4. Kinetic energy
Show answer

C. Acceleration — A straight v–t graph means velocity changes at a uniform rate, so the acceleration (the slope) is constant. Velocity, displacement and kinetic energy all keep increasing.

Laws Of Motion

Q9. A block of mass 2 kg rests on a horizontal surface with coefficient of static friction 0.4 (g = 10 m/s²). What is the maximum horizontal force that can be applied without the block moving?

  1. 4 N
  2. 8 N
  3. 20 N
  4. 0.8 N
Show answer

B. 8 N — Maximum static friction = μmg = 0.4 × 2 × 10 = 8 N. Any applied force up to 8 N is balanced by friction, so the block stays at rest.

Work Energy Momentum

Q10. Two objects of masses m and 4m have equal kinetic energies. What is the ratio of the magnitudes of their linear momenta (lighter : heavier)?

  1. 1 : 4
  2. 1 : 2
  3. 2 : 1
  4. 1 : 1
Show answer

B. 1 : 2 — Since p = √(2mKE) and KE is equal, p ∝ √m. Ratio = √m : √(4m) = 1 : 2.

Gravitation

Q11. A satellite orbits Earth in a circular orbit. If it is moved to a higher circular orbit, its orbital speed:

  1. Increases
  2. Decreases
  3. Stays the same
  4. Becomes zero
Show answer

B. Decreases — Orbital speed v = √(GM/r), which decreases as the orbital radius r increases. A higher orbit therefore has a smaller orbital speed.

Elasticity

Q12. A wire of length L and cross-sectional area A stretches by ΔL under a load F. If a second wire of the same material has twice the length but the same area and load, its extension will be:

  1. ΔL/2
  2. ΔL
  3. 2ΔL
  4. 4ΔL
Show answer

C. 2ΔL — From Y = FL/(AΔL), extension ΔL = FL/(AY) is proportional to length L. Doubling L doubles the extension to 2ΔL.

Surface Tension

Q13. A liquid drop of radius R is broken into 8 identical smaller drops. Compared with the original drop, the total surface area of the smaller drops is:

  1. Half
  2. The same
  3. Twice
  4. Four times
Show answer

C. Twice — Volume is conserved: 8 × (4/3)πr³ = (4/3)πR³ gives r = R/2. Total area = 8 × 4π(R/2)² = 8πR², which is twice the original 4πR².

Thermodynamics

Q14. An ideal gas undergoes a process in which its pressure is doubled while its temperature is kept constant. By what factor does its volume change?

  1. Doubles
  2. Halves
  3. Stays the same
  4. Becomes four times
Show answer

B. Halves — At constant temperature, Boyle's law gives PV = constant. Doubling the pressure halves the volume.

Thermodynamics

Q15. In an adiabatic process for an ideal gas, which of the following is true?

  1. No heat is exchanged with the surroundings
  2. Temperature remains constant
  3. Pressure remains constant
  4. Internal energy remains constant
Show answer

A. No heat is exchanged with the surroundings — An adiabatic process is defined by zero heat exchange (Q = 0). Temperature, pressure and internal energy generally all change during it.

Oscillations

Q16. The bob of a simple pendulum is given a small displacement and released. If the length of the pendulum is quadrupled, its time period becomes:

  1. Half
  2. Twice
  3. Four times
  4. Unchanged
Show answer

B. Twice — For a simple pendulum T = 2π√(L/g), so T ∝ √L. Quadrupling L multiplies the period by √4 = 2.

Waves

Q17. A sound wave travels from air into water. Which property of the wave necessarily remains unchanged?

  1. Speed
  2. Wavelength
  3. Frequency
  4. Amplitude
Show answer

C. Frequency — Frequency is set by the source and does not change when a wave crosses into a new medium. Speed and wavelength both change (and amplitude generally changes too).

Electrostatics

Q18. Two point charges of +2 μC and +2 μC are separated by a distance r. If the distance between them is halved, the electrostatic force between them becomes:

  1. Half
  2. Double
  3. Four times
  4. One-fourth
Show answer

C. Four times — Coulomb's force F ∝ 1/r². Halving the separation multiplies the force by (1/(1/2)²) = 4, so it becomes four times as large.

Current Electricity

Q19. Three resistors each of 6 Ω are connected in parallel. The equivalent resistance of the combination is:

  1. 18 Ω
  2. 6 Ω
  3. 3 Ω
  4. 2 Ω
Show answer

D. 2 Ω — For equal resistors in parallel, R_eq = R/n = 6/3 = 2 Ω.

Magnetism

Q20. A straight conductor carries a steady current. The magnetic field at a fixed perpendicular distance from the wire is directly proportional to:

  1. The current in the wire
  2. The square of the current
  3. The cross-sectional area of the wire
  4. The resistance of the wire
Show answer

A. The current in the wire — By Ampère's law, B = μ₀I/(2πr) for a long straight wire, so B is directly proportional to the current I.

Optics

Q21. Light of wavelength 500 nm in vacuum enters a medium of refractive index 1.5. Its wavelength in the medium is approximately:

  1. 500 nm
  2. 750 nm
  3. 333 nm
  4. 250 nm
Show answer

C. 333 nm — Frequency is unchanged, so wavelength in the medium = λ₀/n = 500/1.5 ≈ 333 nm.

Modern Physics

Q22. In the photoelectric effect, increasing the intensity of incident light (while keeping its frequency fixed above the threshold) increases:

  1. The maximum kinetic energy of emitted electrons
  2. The number of emitted electrons per second
  3. The threshold frequency of the metal
  4. The work function of the metal
Show answer

B. The number of emitted electrons per second — Higher intensity means more photons per second, so more electrons are ejected per second (larger photocurrent). Maximum kinetic energy depends on frequency, not intensity; threshold frequency and work function are properties of the metal.
