Electrostatics

Q1. The SI unit of electric charge is the:

  1. Ampere
  2. Coulomb
  3. Volt
  4. Farad
Show answer

B. Coulomb — Electric charge is measured in coulombs (C), where 1 C = 1 A·s. The ampere is current, the volt is potential difference, and the farad is capacitance.

Electromagnetic Induction

Q2. The production of an induced EMF in a coil due to a changing magnetic flux through it is called:

  1. Refraction
  2. Resonance
  3. Electromagnetic induction
  4. Polarisation
Show answer

C. Electromagnetic induction — Faraday's law describes electromagnetic induction: a changing magnetic flux through a circuit induces an EMF proportional to the rate of change of flux.

Alternating Current

Q3. A device used to step up or step down an alternating (AC) voltage is a:

  1. Transformer
  2. Rectifier
  3. Capacitor
  4. Resistor
Show answer

A. Transformer — A transformer changes AC voltage using electromagnetic induction between two coils. A rectifier converts AC to DC; it cannot step voltage up or down.

Electromagnetic Waves

Q4. In the electromagnetic spectrum, which of these has the longest wavelength?

  1. Gamma rays
  2. X-rays
  3. Visible light
  4. Radio waves
Show answer

D. Radio waves — Radio waves have the longest wavelength (and lowest frequency). Gamma rays have the shortest wavelength and highest frequency.

Electrostatics

Q5. According to Coulomb's law, the electrostatic force between two point charges is inversely proportional to the:

  1. Distance between them
  2. Square of the distance between them
  3. Cube of the distance between them
  4. Product of the charges
Show answer

B. Square of the distance between them — Coulomb's law: F = kq₁q₂/r². The force is directly proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance r between them.

Optics

Q6. The type of lens used to correct myopia (short-sightedness) is a:

  1. Convex lens
  2. Concave lens
  3. Cylindrical lens
  4. Bifocal lens
Show answer

B. Concave lens — Myopia (short-sightedness) is corrected with a concave (diverging) lens, which moves the image back onto the retina. Hypermetropia is corrected with a convex lens.

Electrostatics Conductors

Q7. The electric field just inside a charged, isolated conductor in electrostatic equilibrium is:

  1. Zero everywhere inside
  2. Maximum at the centre
  3. Equal to the field just outside
  4. Directed towards the surface
Show answer

A. Zero everywhere inside — In electrostatic equilibrium, charges reside on the surface of a conductor and the net electric field everywhere inside the conductor material is zero.

Capacitance Dielectrics

Q8. When a dielectric slab is inserted to completely fill the gap between the plates of an isolated (disconnected from any battery) charged parallel-plate capacitor, the potential difference across the plates:

  1. Increases
  2. Decreases
  3. Remains unchanged
  4. Becomes zero
Show answer

B. Decreases — For an isolated capacitor the charge Q is fixed. Inserting a dielectric increases capacitance C, and since V = Q/C, the potential difference decreases.

Current Electricity Drift

Q9. The drift velocity of free electrons in a metallic conductor carrying a steady current is typically of the order of:

  1. 10^8 m/s
  2. 10^6 m/s
  3. 10^3 m/s
  4. 10^-4 m/s
Show answer

D. 10^-4 m/s — Although electrons move randomly at high speeds, the net drift velocity in the direction of the field is very small, of the order of 10^-4 m/s (a fraction of a mm per second).

Magnetism Lorentz Force

Q10. A charged particle moving with velocity v enters a uniform magnetic field B such that v is exactly parallel to B. The magnetic force on the particle is:

  1. Maximum and along v
  2. Zero
  3. Maximum and perpendicular to v
  4. Equal to qvB directed along B
Show answer

B. Zero — The magnetic force is F = qv×B = qvB sinθ. When v is parallel to B, θ = 0, so sinθ = 0 and the force is zero.

Magnetism Materials

Q11. A material that is weakly repelled by an external magnetic field and has a relative permeability slightly less than 1 is classified as:

  1. Ferromagnetic
  2. Paramagnetic
  3. Diamagnetic
  4. Ferrimagnetic
Show answer

C. Diamagnetic — Diamagnetic materials are weakly repelled by magnetic fields and have relative permeability slightly less than 1 (magnetic susceptibility small and negative).

Emi Lenz Law

Q12. According to Lenz's law, the direction of an induced current in a circuit is always such that it:

  1. Aids the change in magnetic flux that produces it
  2. Opposes the change in magnetic flux that produces it
  3. Is independent of the change in flux
  4. Is always clockwise when viewed from the source
Show answer

B. Opposes the change in magnetic flux that produces it — Lenz's law states the induced current opposes the change in magnetic flux causing it, which is a consequence of conservation of energy.

Ac Resonance

Q13. In a series LCR circuit driven at its resonant frequency, the power factor of the circuit is:

  1. Zero
  2. 0.5
  3. 1
  4. Between 0 and 0.5
Show answer

C. 1 — At resonance the inductive and capacitive reactances cancel, the impedance is purely resistive, current and voltage are in phase, so the power factor cosφ = 1.

Em Waves Structure

Q14. In an electromagnetic wave travelling through vacuum, the electric field vector E and the magnetic field vector B are oriented such that they are:

  1. Parallel to each other and to the direction of propagation
  2. Mutually perpendicular and both perpendicular to the direction of propagation
  3. Perpendicular to each other but parallel to the direction of propagation
  4. Antiparallel to each other
Show answer

B. Mutually perpendicular and both perpendicular to the direction of propagation — In an EM wave, E and B are perpendicular to each other and each is perpendicular to the direction of propagation, making it a transverse wave.

Ray Optics Tir

Q15. Total internal reflection of light can occur only when light travels from:

  1. A rarer medium to a denser medium at any angle
  2. A denser medium to a rarer medium at an angle greater than the critical angle
  3. A rarer medium to a denser medium at the critical angle
  4. A denser medium to a rarer medium at an angle less than the critical angle
Show answer

B. A denser medium to a rarer medium at an angle greater than the critical angle — Total internal reflection requires light going from a denser to a rarer medium with the angle of incidence exceeding the critical angle, so no refracted ray escapes.

Wave Optics Interference

Q16. In Young's double-slit experiment, if the separation between the two slits is increased while all else is kept constant, the fringe width of the interference pattern:

  1. Increases
  2. Decreases
  3. Remains the same
  4. First increases then decreases
Show answer

B. Decreases — Fringe width β = λD/d is inversely proportional to the slit separation d, so increasing d decreases the fringe width.

Dual Nature Photoelectric

Q17. In the photoelectric effect, increasing the intensity of incident light (while keeping its frequency above the threshold) increases:

  1. The maximum kinetic energy of emitted electrons
  2. The number of electrons emitted per second
  3. The threshold frequency of the metal
  4. The stopping potential
Show answer

B. The number of electrons emitted per second — Higher intensity means more photons per second, so more electrons are ejected per second (larger photocurrent). Maximum kinetic energy and stopping potential depend only on frequency, not intensity.

Atoms Bohr Model

Q18. The energy of the electron in the second orbit (n = 2) of a hydrogen atom, given the ground-state energy is -13.6 eV, is:

  1. -13.6 eV
  2. -6.8 eV
  3. -3.4 eV
  4. -1.51 eV
Show answer

C. -3.4 eV — In the Bohr model E_n = -13.6/n^2 eV. For n = 2, E = -13.6/4 = -3.4 eV.

Nuclei Binding Energy

Q19. The binding energy per nucleon is greatest for nuclei with mass number around 56 (iron region). This implies that these nuclei are:

  1. The most unstable of all nuclei
  2. The most stable, being hardest to break apart per nucleon
  3. Radioactive with the shortest half-lives
  4. Capable of neither fusion nor fission
Show answer

B. The most stable, being hardest to break apart per nucleon — A higher binding energy per nucleon means the nucleons are more tightly bound, so nuclei near iron (A≈56) are the most stable.

Semiconductors Pn Junction

Q20. In a p-n junction diode, the depletion region is a layer near the junction that is:

  1. Rich in free charge carriers
  2. Depleted of free charge carriers and contains immobile ions
  3. A perfect conductor
  4. Made only of free electrons
Show answer

B. Depleted of free charge carriers and contains immobile ions — Diffusion of carriers across the junction leaves behind immobile charged ions and removes free carriers, forming a depletion region with an associated potential barrier.

Ray Optics Lens Combination

Q21. A thin convex lens of power +5 D is placed in contact with a thin concave lens of power -2 D. The focal length of the combination is:

  1. +33.3 cm (converging)
  2. -33.3 cm (diverging)
  3. +14.3 cm (converging)
  4. +50 cm (converging)
Show answer

A. +33.3 cm (converging) — For thin lenses in contact, net power P = P1 + P2 = 5 + (-2) = +3 D. Focal length f = 1/P = 1/3 m ≈ +33.3 cm, and being positive it is converging.

Electrostatics Potential

Q22. The work done in moving a test charge between two points that lie on the same equipotential surface is:

  1. Maximum
  2. Zero
  3. Negative
  4. Equal to the charge times the field
Show answer

B. Zero — On an equipotential surface the potential difference between any two points is zero, so W = qΔV = 0; no work is done in moving a charge along it.
