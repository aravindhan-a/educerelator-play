Banking

Q1. The Reserve Bank of India (RBI), the country's central bank, was established in the year:

  1. 1935
  2. 1947
  3. 1950
  4. 1969
Show answer

A. 1935 — The RBI was established on 1 April 1935 under the RBI Act, 1934, and was nationalised in 1949. 1969 was the year of the first bank nationalisation.

Planning

Q2. The body that replaced the Planning Commission of India in 2015 is:

  1. The Finance Commission
  2. NITI Aayog
  3. The RBI
  4. SEBI
Show answer

B. NITI Aayog — NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India) replaced the Planning Commission on 1 January 2015 as the government's policy think-tank.

Economy

Q3. In economics, 'GDP' stands for:

  1. Gross Domestic Product
  2. General Domestic Produce
  3. Gross Development Programme
  4. Global Domestic Product
Show answer

A. Gross Domestic Product — GDP is Gross Domestic Product — the total monetary value of all final goods and services produced within a country's borders in a given period.

Economy

Q4. The Goods and Services Tax (GST) came into effect in India in the year:

  1. 2014
  2. 2016
  3. 2017
  4. 2019
Show answer

C. 2017 — GST was launched on 1 July 2017, replacing many indirect taxes with a single unified tax under the slogan 'One Nation, One Tax'.

Banking

Q5. Which institution has the sole authority to issue currency notes in India (except one-rupee notes)?

  1. State Bank of India
  2. Ministry of Finance
  3. Reserve Bank of India
  4. NABARD
Show answer

C. Reserve Bank of India — The RBI is the sole issuer of banknotes in India (the one-rupee note and coins are issued by the Government of India, though circulated by the RBI).

Economy

Q6. The 'fiscal deficit' of a government is the excess of its:

  1. Total expenditure over total receipts excluding borrowings
  2. Revenue over expenditure
  3. Exports over imports
  4. Imports over exports
Show answer

A. Total expenditure over total receipts excluding borrowings — The fiscal deficit is the amount by which the government's total expenditure exceeds its total receipts excluding borrowings — i.e. how much it needs to borrow. The gap between exports and imports is the trade deficit.

National Income Accounting

Q7. The difference between a country's Gross National Product (GNP) and its Gross Domestic Product (GDP) is accounted for by:

  1. Net indirect taxes
  2. Depreciation of capital
  3. Net factor income from abroad
  4. Subsidies on production
Show answer

C. Net factor income from abroad — GNP = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (income earned by residents abroad minus income earned by foreigners domestically), so NFIA is exactly what separates the two aggregates.

Monetary Policy

Q8. An increase in the repo rate by the Reserve Bank of India is generally intended to:

  1. Increase liquidity and boost growth
  2. Reduce liquidity in the system and help control inflation
  3. Directly finance the government's fiscal deficit
  4. Guarantee an appreciation of the rupee
Show answer

B. Reduce liquidity in the system and help control inflation — A higher repo rate makes RBI borrowing costlier for banks, raising lending rates and squeezing liquidity, which dampens demand and helps contain inflation.

Monetary Policy

Q9. Which of the following best describes the Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)?

  1. The rate at which the RBI lends to commercial banks
  2. The share of deposits banks must invest in government securities
  3. The minimum ratio of a bank's capital to its risk-weighted assets
  4. The share of net demand and time liabilities banks must hold as cash reserves with the RBI
Show answer

D. The share of net demand and time liabilities banks must hold as cash reserves with the RBI — CRR is the fraction of net demand and time liabilities (NDTL) that banks must keep as cash reserves with the RBI; the other options describe the repo rate, the SLR concept, and the capital adequacy ratio.

Inflation Measurement

Q10. India's flexible inflation targeting framework requires the RBI to keep inflation, measured by which index, within a specified band?

  1. Consumer Price Index (Combined)
  2. Wholesale Price Index
  3. GDP deflator
  4. Index of Industrial Production
Show answer

A. Consumer Price Index (Combined) — The monetary policy framework anchors the inflation target on the Consumer Price Index (Combined), not the WPI or the GDP deflator; the IIP measures industrial output, not prices.

Fiscal Policy

Q11. Primary deficit in the Union Budget is defined as:

  1. Fiscal deficit plus interest payments
  2. Revenue deficit minus grants for capital assets
  3. Fiscal deficit minus interest payments
  4. Total expenditure minus total receipts including borrowings
Show answer

C. Fiscal deficit minus interest payments — Primary deficit equals fiscal deficit minus interest payments; it isolates the current year's borrowing need after removing the burden of past debt servicing.

Taxation

Q12. Which of the following is a direct tax?

  1. Goods and Services Tax
  2. Customs duty
  3. Union excise duty
  4. Corporation tax
Show answer

D. Corporation tax — Corporation tax is levied directly on company profits and its burden cannot be shifted, making it a direct tax; GST, customs, and excise are indirect taxes on goods and services.

Money Supply

Q13. In India, narrow money (M1) consists of:

  1. Currency with the public plus time deposits with banks
  2. Currency with the public plus demand deposits with the banking system plus other deposits with the RBI
  3. Demand deposits plus time deposits with banks
  4. Currency with the public plus total bank deposits
Show answer

B. Currency with the public plus demand deposits with the banking system plus other deposits with the RBI — M1 (narrow money) = currency with the public + demand deposits with the banking system + other deposits with the RBI. Time deposits are added only in the broader aggregate M3.

Balance Of Payments

Q14. Which of the following transactions is recorded in the current account of India's balance of payments?

  1. Import of crude oil
  2. Foreign direct investment inflow
  3. External commercial borrowing
  4. Repayment of foreign loan principal
Show answer

A. Import of crude oil — Merchandise trade such as the import of crude oil is a current account item, whereas FDI, external commercial borrowing, and loan repayments are capital/financial account transactions.

Banking

Q15. Under RBI norms, which of the following is classified as 'priority sector' lending?

  1. Loans to large corporate houses
  2. Personal loans for luxury consumption
  3. Agriculture and allied activities
  4. Speculative real estate purchases
Show answer

C. Agriculture and allied activities — Priority sector lending covers areas such as agriculture, micro and small enterprises, education, and weaker sections that need adequate credit; large corporate and speculative loans are excluded.

Financial Institutions

Q16. The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) is primarily responsible for:

  1. Managing the country's foreign exchange reserves
  2. Regulating the securities (stock) market
  3. Setting the policy repo rate
  4. Regulating the insurance industry
Show answer

B. Regulating the securities (stock) market — SEBI is the statutory regulator of the securities market, protecting investors and overseeing exchanges. Forex reserves and the repo rate fall to the RBI, and insurance is regulated by IRDAI.

Inequality Poverty

Q17. The Gini coefficient is used as a measure of:

  1. The rate of inflation
  2. The level of unemployment
  3. The rate of economic growth
  4. Inequality in the distribution of income or wealth
Show answer

D. Inequality in the distribution of income or wealth — The Gini coefficient ranges from 0 (perfect equality) to 1 (perfect inequality) and quantifies how unequally income or wealth is distributed across a population.

Agriculture Economics

Q18. In India, the Minimum Support Prices (MSP) for major crops are recommended by:

  1. Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP)
  2. Food Corporation of India
  3. NITI Aayog
  4. Reserve Bank of India
Show answer

A. Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP) — The CACP recommends MSPs to the government based on cost of production and other factors; the FCI handles procurement and storage, not price recommendation.

Exchange Rate

Q19. Other things remaining equal, a depreciation of the Indian rupee against the US dollar tends to make:

  1. Both Indian exports and imports cheaper
  2. Indian exports costlier and imports cheaper
  3. Indian exports cheaper and imports costlier
  4. No change in the prices of exports or imports
Show answer

C. Indian exports cheaper and imports costlier — When the rupee depreciates, foreign buyers pay less in their currency for Indian goods (exports cheaper), while Indians pay more rupees for foreign goods (imports costlier).

Planning History

Q20. The strategy of rapid industrialization based on heavy industries in India's Second Five Year Plan was associated with the model developed by:

  1. Amartya Sen
  2. P. C. Mahalanobis
  3. Jagdish Bhagwati
  4. Manmohan Singh
Show answer

B. P. C. Mahalanobis — The Second Five Year Plan followed the Mahalanobis model, emphasizing investment in heavy and capital-goods industries to build a self-reliant industrial base.

Money Market

Q21. Treasury Bills issued by the Government of India are:

  1. Short-term instruments with a maturity of less than one year
  2. Long-term bonds maturing after ten years
  3. Equity shares issued by public sector banks
  4. Instruments that can be purchased only by foreign investors
Show answer

A. Short-term instruments with a maturity of less than one year — Treasury Bills are short-term money-market instruments issued at a discount with maturities of up to one year (91, 182, and 364 days); they are debt, not equity.

Public Finance

Q22. Which of the following is an example of capital expenditure in the Union Budget?

  1. Payment of salaries to government employees
  2. Interest payments on public debt
  3. Food and fertilizer subsidies
  4. Construction of a new national highway
Show answer

D. Construction of a new national highway — Building a highway creates a physical asset, so it is capital expenditure; salaries, interest payments, and subsidies are recurring revenue expenditures that create no lasting asset.
