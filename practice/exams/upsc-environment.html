Ecology

Q1. Which rainforest is often called the 'lungs of the Earth'?

  1. The Sundarbans
  2. The Amazon rainforest
  3. The Western Ghats
  4. The Taiga
Show answer

B. The Amazon rainforest — The Amazon rainforest is called the 'lungs of the Earth' because it produces a large share of the world's oxygen and absorbs vast amounts of carbon dioxide.

Conservation

Q2. 'Project Tiger', India's tiger-conservation programme, was launched in the year:

  1. 1973
  2. 1985
  3. 1992
  4. 2005
Show answer

A. 1973 — Project Tiger was launched in 1973 to protect the Bengal tiger and its habitat through a network of tiger reserves.

Ecology

Q3. The main greenhouse gas responsible for global warming, released by burning fossil fuels, is:

  1. Oxygen
  2. Nitrogen
  3. Carbon dioxide
  4. Argon
Show answer

C. Carbon dioxide — Carbon dioxide (CO₂) is the principal greenhouse gas from fossil-fuel combustion. Oxygen, nitrogen and argon are not significant greenhouse gases.

Ecology

Q4. Depletion of the ozone layer in the stratosphere is caused mainly by:

  1. Carbon dioxide
  2. Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)
  3. Methane
  4. Oxygen
Show answer

B. Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) — Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) release chlorine atoms that destroy ozone molecules, thinning the protective ozone layer. Their use is phased out under the Montreal Protocol.

Conservation

Q5. The national park in Assam famous for protecting the one-horned rhinoceros is:

  1. Gir National Park
  2. Jim Corbett National Park
  3. Kaziranga National Park
  4. Ranthambore National Park
Show answer

C. Kaziranga National Park — Kaziranga National Park in Assam is a UNESCO World Heritage Site home to about two-thirds of the world's one-horned rhinoceroses. Gir is known for the Asiatic lion; Corbett and Ranthambore for tigers.

Ecology

Q6. A community of living organisms together with their physical environment, interacting as a system, is called a(n):

  1. Population
  2. Ecosystem
  3. Biome only
  4. Atmosphere
Show answer

B. Ecosystem — An ecosystem is a community of interacting organisms (biotic) together with their non-living surroundings (abiotic), through which energy flows and nutrients cycle.

Energy Flow

Q7. In an ecological pyramid, the amount of energy transferred from one trophic level to the next is approximately what fraction of the energy available at the lower level?

  1. About 50%
  2. About 10%
  3. About 25%
  4. About 90%
Show answer

B. About 10% — By the ten percent law of Lindeman, only about 10% of the energy at one trophic level is passed on to the next; the rest is lost mainly as metabolic heat.

Biodiversity Concepts

Q8. Which of the following best describes a 'keystone species'?

  1. The species with the largest population in an ecosystem
  2. A species whose impact on its community is disproportionately large relative to its abundance
  3. Any species introduced from another region
  4. The species occupying the highest trophic level
Show answer

B. A species whose impact on its community is disproportionately large relative to its abundance — A keystone species exerts an effect on ecosystem structure far greater than its numbers would suggest; its removal can cause major changes or collapse of the community.

Pollution

Q9. Eutrophication of a water body is most directly caused by the excessive enrichment of which nutrients?

  1. Nitrogen and phosphorus
  2. Calcium and magnesium
  3. Sulphur and chlorine
  4. Iron and manganese
Show answer

A. Nitrogen and phosphorus — Nutrient enrichment, chiefly by nitrogen and phosphorus from fertilizers and sewage, triggers algal blooms whose decomposition depletes dissolved oxygen, defining eutrophication.

Conservation Status

Q10. Under the IUCN Red List, which category indicates the highest risk for a species that still survives in the wild?

  1. Vulnerable
  2. Endangered
  3. Critically Endangered
  4. Near Threatened
Show answer

C. Critically Endangered — Among species still found in the wild, 'Critically Endangered' denotes the highest extinction risk, ranking above Endangered and Vulnerable in the IUCN threat categories.

International Conventions

Q11. The Ramsar Convention is an international treaty concerned primarily with the conservation of which type of ecosystem?

  1. Coral reefs
  2. Tropical rainforests
  3. Wetlands
  4. Deserts
Show answer

C. Wetlands — The Ramsar Convention (1971) provides for the conservation and wise use of wetlands and designates Wetlands of International Importance, known as Ramsar sites.

Biodiversity Hotspots

Q12. A biodiversity 'hotspot' is defined by two criteria: it must contain a high number of endemic plant species and it must have

  1. Lost at least 70% of its original habitat
  2. Been declared a protected area
  3. A high average annual rainfall
  4. At least one large national park
Show answer

A. Lost at least 70% of its original habitat — A hotspot must have at least 1,500 endemic vascular plant species and have lost 70% or more of its original primary vegetation, marking it as both rich and threatened.

Climate Change

Q13. Which pair of gases from the following contributes most to the greenhouse effect by trapping outgoing longwave (infrared) radiation?

  1. Nitrogen and oxygen
  2. Carbon dioxide and methane
  3. Argon and neon
  4. Hydrogen and helium
Show answer

B. Carbon dioxide and methane — Carbon dioxide and methane absorb and re-emit infrared radiation, warming the atmosphere. Nitrogen, oxygen and the noble gases are not effective greenhouse gases.

Pollution

Q14. The process by which certain persistent toxic substances increase in concentration at successively higher trophic levels of a food chain is called

  1. Bioremediation
  2. Biomagnification
  3. Bioaccumulation within a single organism
  4. Eutrophication
Show answer

B. Biomagnification — Biomagnification is the increasing concentration of a persistent pollutant, such as DDT or mercury, at each higher trophic level. Bioaccumulation refers to build-up within one organism over time.

Conservation Methods

Q15. In situ conservation of biodiversity refers to protecting species

  1. In zoos and botanical gardens
  2. In their natural habitats
  3. In gene banks and seed vaults
  4. Through cryopreservation of gametes
Show answer

B. In their natural habitats — In situ conservation protects species within their natural habitats, as in national parks and biosphere reserves. Zoos, gene banks and seed vaults are examples of ex situ conservation.

Ecology Concepts

Q16. The 'edge effect' in ecology refers to the distinctive conditions and species diversity found

  1. At the boundary between two different ecosystems
  2. At the peak of an ecological pyramid
  3. In the deepest part of an ocean
  4. At the centre of a large forest
Show answer

A. At the boundary between two different ecosystems — The transition zone between two ecosystems is an ecotone, and the tendency for greater species richness and density there is termed the edge effect.

Atmosphere

Q17. Which one of the following is a naturally occurring greenhouse gas that also protects life by absorbing harmful ultraviolet radiation in the stratosphere?

  1. Methane
  2. Ozone
  3. Nitrous oxide
  4. Carbon dioxide
Show answer

B. Ozone — Ozone is a greenhouse gas in the troposphere but in the stratosphere it forms a layer that absorbs most of the Sun's harmful ultraviolet radiation, shielding life on Earth.

Organisms And Environment

Q18. Organisms that can tolerate a wide range of an environmental factor such as temperature are described as

  1. Stenothermal
  2. Eurythermal
  3. Stenohaline
  4. Euryhaline
Show answer

B. Eurythermal — The prefix 'eury' means wide and 'thermal' refers to temperature, so eurythermal organisms tolerate a broad temperature range. 'Steno' denotes a narrow range; 'haline' refers to salinity.

Ecosystem Dynamics

Q19. A food web is more stable than a simple food chain primarily because

  1. It contains only producers
  2. It has fewer species interactions
  3. It offers alternative feeding pathways when one species declines
  4. It transfers 100% of energy between levels
Show answer

C. It offers alternative feeding pathways when one species declines — In a food web, consumers can shift to alternative prey if one food source falls, so the network of interconnected chains gives the ecosystem greater resilience than a single linear chain.

Land And Soil

Q20. Which of the following soil conservation practices involves planting crops along the natural contour lines of a slope to reduce runoff and erosion?

  1. Shifting cultivation
  2. Contour ploughing
  3. Monocropping
  4. Slash-and-burn farming
Show answer

B. Contour ploughing — Contour ploughing follows the contour lines of a slope so that the ridges slow water flow, reducing surface runoff and soil erosion. The other options tend to increase soil loss.

Nutrient Cycling

Q21. The primary ecological role of decomposers such as bacteria and fungi in an ecosystem is to

  1. Fix atmospheric nitrogen into the soil directly
  2. Convert solar energy into chemical energy
  3. Break down dead organic matter and recycle nutrients
  4. Occupy the top of the food chain
Show answer

C. Break down dead organic matter and recycle nutrients — Decomposers break down dead plants and animals, releasing nutrients back into the soil and atmosphere so producers can reuse them, thus closing nutrient cycles.

Wetlands

Q22. Wetlands are often called the 'kidneys' of a landscape mainly because they

  1. Store the largest share of the world's freshwater
  2. Filter pollutants and purify water passing through them
  3. Are the primary source of atmospheric oxygen
  4. Prevent all forms of coastal erosion
Show answer

B. Filter pollutants and purify water passing through them — Wetlands trap sediments and absorb or break down nutrients and pollutants as water flows through them, filtering and purifying it much as kidneys filter blood, hence the nickname.
