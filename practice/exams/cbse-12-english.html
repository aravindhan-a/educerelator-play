Literary Devices

Q1. Identify the figure of speech: 'The classroom was a zoo.'

  1. Simile
  2. Metaphor
  3. Hyperbole
  4. Alliteration
Show answer

B. Metaphor — It is a metaphor — a direct comparison WITHOUT 'like' or 'as' (the classroom is called a zoo).

Vocabulary

Q2. Choose the antonym of 'optimist':

  1. dreamer
  2. pessimist
  3. realist
  4. idealist
Show answer

B. pessimist — An optimist expects good outcomes; the opposite is a pessimist, who expects the worst.

Grammar

Q3. A word that describes or modifies a verb is called a/an:

  1. Adjective
  2. Adverb
  3. Noun
  4. Pronoun
Show answer

B. Adverb — An adverb modifies a verb, adjective, or another adverb (e.g. 'ran quickly'). An adjective modifies a noun.

Vocabulary

Q4. Choose the synonym of 'diligent':

  1. lazy
  2. hardworking
  3. careless
  4. slow
Show answer

B. hardworking — 'Diligent' means showing careful and persistent effort — that is, hardworking.

Grammar

Q5. Choose the correct reported speech: She said, 'I will call you tomorrow.'

  1. She said that she would call me the next day.
  2. She said that I will call you tomorrow.
  3. She says she will call me tomorrow.
  4. She said that she will call me tomorrow.
Show answer

A. She said that she would call me the next day. — In reported speech 'will' → 'would' and 'tomorrow' → 'the next day', with pronouns adjusted: 'She said that she would call me the next day.'

Literary Devices

Q6. Identify the figure of speech: 'Peter Piper picked a peck of pickled peppers.'

  1. Alliteration
  2. Metaphor
  3. Simile
  4. Personification
Show answer

A. Alliteration — The repetition of the initial 'p' sound in nearby words is alliteration.

Vocabulary

Q7. The word 'meticulous' most nearly means:

  1. careless
  2. very careful and precise
  3. generous
  4. confused
Show answer

B. very careful and precise — 'Meticulous' means showing great attention to detail — very careful and precise.

Grammar

Q8. Choose the correct passive form: 'They will complete the project.'

  1. The project will be completed by them.
  2. The project is completed by them.
  3. The project was completed by them.
  4. They will be completed the project.
Show answer

A. The project will be completed by them. — Future simple passive is 'will be + past participle': 'The project will be completed by them.'

Literary Devices

Q9. 'I have told you a million times' is an example of:

  1. Hyperbole
  2. Simile
  3. Irony
  4. Metaphor
Show answer

A. Hyperbole — It is a deliberate exaggeration for emphasis — hyperbole (you have not literally said it a million times).

Grammar

Q10. Which word is a conjunction in: 'She was tired but she kept working.'?

  1. tired
  2. but
  3. working
  4. she
Show answer

B. but — 'But' joins the two clauses, so it is the conjunction. Conjunctions connect words, phrases or clauses.

Reported Speech

Q11. Choose the correct indirect (reported) speech: She said to him, "Do you like coffee?"

  1. She asked him if he liked coffee.
  2. She asked him do you like coffee.
  3. She said him that he likes coffee.
  4. She asked him that did he like coffee.
Show answer

A. She asked him if he liked coffee. — A yes/no question in reported speech is introduced by 'if/whether', the tense is back-shifted (like → liked), and the question mark and question word-order are dropped.

Active Passive Voice

Q12. Choose the correct passive form of: "Someone has stolen my watch."

  1. My watch was stolen by someone.
  2. My watch has stolen.
  3. My watch is being stolen.
  4. My watch has been stolen.
Show answer

D. My watch has been stolen. — The present perfect active 'has stolen' becomes 'has been + past participle' in the passive; the indefinite agent 'by someone' is normally omitted.

Vocabulary Synonyms

Q13. Choose the word that is the SYNONYM of 'candid'.

  1. frank
  2. secretive
  3. careless
  4. hostile
Show answer

A. frank — 'Candid' means open and truthful, so 'frank' is the closest synonym; 'secretive' is its opposite.

Vocabulary Antonyms

Q14. Choose the word that is the ANTONYM of 'scarce'.

  1. rare
  2. meagre
  3. abundant
  4. limited
Show answer

C. abundant — 'Scarce' means in short supply; its opposite is 'abundant' (plentiful). The other options are near-synonyms of scarce.

Figures Of Speech

Q15. Identify the figure of speech: "The flowers danced in the breeze."

  1. Simile
  2. Metaphor
  3. Personification
  4. Hyperbole
Show answer

C. Personification — Flowers are given the human action of 'dancing', so a non-human thing is described as having human qualities — that is personification.

Idioms

Q16. What does the idiom 'to bury the hatchet' mean?

  1. To dig a deep hole
  2. To hide a weapon
  3. To start a fresh quarrel
  4. To make peace and end a quarrel
Show answer

D. To make peace and end a quarrel — 'To bury the hatchet' means to settle a dispute and become friendly again, i.e., to make peace.

Determiners

Q17. Fill in the blank with the correct determiner: "There is very ____ water left in the bottle."

  1. few
  2. little
  3. many
  4. fewer
Show answer

B. little — 'Water' is an uncountable noun, so the quantifier 'little' is used; 'few/many/fewer' are used only with countable nouns.

Modals

Q18. Choose the modal that best expresses strong obligation: "You ____ wear a helmet while riding a motorcycle; it is the law."

  1. may
  2. might
  3. could
  4. must
Show answer

D. must — 'Must' expresses strong necessity or obligation, which suits a legal requirement; 'may/might/could' express possibility or permission, not obligation.

Prepositions

Q19. Fill in the correct preposition: "She has been living in Delhi ____ 2015."

  1. since
  2. for
  3. from
  4. by
Show answer

A. since — With the present perfect continuous, 'since' is used before a specific point of time (2015), while 'for' is used with a length of time.

Tenses

Q20. Choose the correct verb form: "By the time we reached the platform, the train ____."

  1. has already left
  2. was already leaving
  3. had already left
  4. have already left
Show answer

C. had already left — The past perfect 'had already left' shows an action completed before another past action (reaching the platform).

Conjunctions

Q21. Choose the correct conjunction: "____ it was raining heavily, they continued the match."

  1. Although
  2. Because
  3. So
  4. Unless
Show answer

A. Although — The two clauses express a contrast, so the concessive conjunction 'although' is correct; 'because' and 'so' show cause/effect and 'unless' shows condition.

Flamingo Poetry

Q22. The line "A thing of beauty is a joy for ever" opens a poem in Flamingo written by which poet?

  1. Pablo Neruda
  2. Robert Frost
  3. Adrienne Rich
  4. John Keats
Show answer

D. John Keats — The poem 'A Thing of Beauty' is an extract from John Keats's 'Endymion'.

Flamingo Prose

Q23. In 'The Last Lesson', the villagers and students are ordered to learn which language in place of French?

  1. English
  2. Latin
  3. German
  4. Italian
Show answer

C. German — After Prussia occupied Alsace and Lorraine, an order came that only German, not French, would be taught in the schools.

Flamingo Poetry

Q24. In Adrienne Rich's 'Aunt Jennifer's Tigers', the tigers chiefly symbolise:

  1. Aunt Jennifer's fear of animals
  2. Her husband's wealth
  3. The fearless freedom that Aunt Jennifer herself lacks
  4. The passage of time
Show answer

C. The fearless freedom that Aunt Jennifer herself lacks — The 'prancing, proud and unafraid' tigers embody the fearlessness and freedom denied to the oppressed Aunt Jennifer in her constrained married life.

Writing Skills Notice

Q25. Which of the following is an essential feature of the format of a formal NOTICE?

  1. A complimentary close such as 'Yours sincerely'
  2. The heading 'NOTICE', the date, and the name of the issuing authority
  3. A fixed word limit of exactly 200 words
  4. A salutation such as 'Respected Sir'
Show answer

B. The heading 'NOTICE', the date, and the name of the issuing authority — A notice must carry the word 'NOTICE', the date of issue, and the signature/name of the issuing authority; salutations and complimentary closes belong to letters, not notices.

Figures Of Speech

Q26. Identify the figure of speech: "Life is a journey with many turns."

  1. Simile
  2. Personification
  3. Alliteration
  4. Metaphor
Show answer

D. Metaphor — Life is directly called a journey without using 'like' or 'as', so it is a metaphor; a simile would require an explicit comparison word.
