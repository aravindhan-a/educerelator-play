Human Physiology

Q1. The structural and functional unit of the kidney is the:

  1. Neuron
  2. Nephron
  3. Alveolus
  4. Villus
Show answer

B. Nephron — The nephron is the functional unit of the kidney; each kidney has about a million nephrons that filter blood and form urine.

Reproduction

Q2. In humans, fertilisation normally takes place in the:

  1. Uterus
  2. Ovary
  3. Fallopian tube (oviduct)
  4. Vagina
Show answer

C. Fallopian tube (oviduct) — Fertilisation of the ovum by the sperm occurs in the ampulla of the fallopian tube (oviduct); the embryo then implants in the uterus.

Genetics

Q3. Who proposed the double-helix model of DNA?

  1. Watson and Crick
  2. Mendel and Morgan
  3. Darwin and Wallace
  4. Hooke and Schleiden
Show answer

A. Watson and Crick — James Watson and Francis Crick proposed the double-helix structure of DNA in 1953, using X-ray data from Rosalind Franklin.

Genetics

Q4. Mendel's Law of Segregation states that:

  1. Genes are always linked together
  2. Alleles of a gene separate during gamete formation
  3. Acquired traits are inherited
  4. Only dominant traits are passed on
Show answer

B. Alleles of a gene separate during gamete formation — The Law of Segregation: the two alleles of a gene separate during gamete formation, so each gamete carries only one allele.

Cell Biology

Q5. Which type of cell division produces gametes and halves the chromosome number?

  1. Mitosis
  2. Meiosis
  3. Binary fission
  4. Budding
Show answer

B. Meiosis — Meiosis produces four haploid gametes from one diploid cell, halving the chromosome number; mitosis keeps the number the same.

Human Physiology

Q6. Which hormone lowers blood glucose levels?

  1. Glucagon
  2. Insulin
  3. Adrenaline
  4. Thyroxine
Show answer

B. Insulin — Insulin, secreted by the β-cells of the pancreas, lowers blood glucose. Glucagon does the opposite (raises it).

Evolution

Q7. The theory of natural selection was proposed by:

  1. Gregor Mendel
  2. Charles Darwin
  3. Louis Pasteur
  4. Jean-Baptiste Lamarck
Show answer

B. Charles Darwin — Charles Darwin proposed evolution by natural selection in 'On the Origin of Species' (1859).

Biotechnology

Q8. A test cross is a cross between an organism with a dominant phenotype and one that is:

  1. Homozygous dominant
  2. Homozygous recessive
  3. Heterozygous
  4. A different species
Show answer

B. Homozygous recessive — A test cross pairs the unknown genotype with a homozygous recessive individual to reveal whether it is homozygous or heterozygous.

Human Physiology

Q9. The largest gland in the human body is the:

  1. Pancreas
  2. Liver
  3. Thyroid
  4. Salivary gland
Show answer

B. Liver — The liver is the largest gland (and largest internal organ) in the human body.

Ecology

Q10. In a food chain, green plants are called:

  1. Producers
  2. Primary consumers
  3. Decomposers
  4. Secondary consumers
Show answer

A. Producers — Green plants make their own food by photosynthesis and form the base of the food chain, so they are producers (autotrophs).

Sexual Reproduction Flowering Plants

Q11. The entry of a pollen tube into the ovule through the micropyle is called:

  1. Porogamy
  2. Chalazogamy
  3. Mesogamy
  4. Apogamy
Show answer

A. Porogamy — Porogamy is pollen-tube entry via the micropyle; chalazogamy is through the chalaza and mesogamy through the integuments.

Sexual Reproduction Flowering Plants

Q12. In a typical angiosperm embryo sac, the primary endosperm nucleus (PEN) formed during double fertilisation is:

  1. Haploid
  2. Diploid
  3. Triploid
  4. Tetraploid
Show answer

C. Triploid — One haploid male gamete fuses with the two haploid polar nuclei (triple fusion), giving a triploid (3n) primary endosperm nucleus.

Human Reproduction

Q13. Ovulation in the human menstrual cycle is triggered by a rapid mid-cycle surge of:

  1. Follicle stimulating hormone
  2. Progesterone
  3. Relaxin
  4. Luteinizing hormone
Show answer

D. Luteinizing hormone — A sharp mid-cycle LH surge induces rupture of the Graafian follicle and release of the ovum (ovulation).

Reproductive Health

Q14. Copper-releasing intrauterine devices (such as Cu-T) prevent conception mainly by:

  1. Suppressing ovulation the way oral pills do
  2. Releasing Cu ions that reduce sperm motility and fertilising capacity
  3. Permanently blocking the fallopian tubes
  4. Stopping the pituitary from secreting gonadotropins
Show answer

B. Releasing Cu ions that reduce sperm motility and fertilising capacity — Copper ions released by Cu-IUDs are spermicidal, suppressing sperm motility and their fertilising capacity; they do not primarily block ovulation or the tubes.

Principles Of Inheritance

Q15. In snapdragon, a cross between red-flowered and white-flowered plants produces pink F1 plants. The F2 phenotypic ratio will be:

  1. 3 red : 1 white
  2. 1 red : 2 pink : 1 white
  3. 9 : 3 : 3 : 1
  4. 2 red : 1 white
Show answer

B. 1 red : 2 pink : 1 white — This is incomplete dominance; heterozygotes are pink, so the F2 genotypic and phenotypic ratios are both 1:2:1 (red : pink : white).

Principles Of Inheritance

Q16. The AB blood group in humans, in which both A and B antigens are fully expressed on the red cells, is an example of:

  1. Incomplete dominance
  2. Epistasis
  3. Pleiotropy
  4. Codominance
Show answer

D. Codominance — Alleles I^A and I^B are codominant: in genotype I^A I^B both antigens appear together rather than blending, which is codominance.

Chromosomal Disorders

Q17. Down's syndrome in humans results from the presence of an extra copy of:

  1. Chromosome 18
  2. The X chromosome
  3. Chromosome 21
  4. The Y chromosome
Show answer

C. Chromosome 21 — Down's syndrome is trisomy 21, an additional copy of chromosome 21 usually caused by non-disjunction during gamete formation.

Molecular Basis Of Inheritance

Q18. During DNA replication, the discontinuously synthesised Okazaki fragments are produced on the:

  1. Lagging strand
  2. Leading strand
  3. Template strand only
  4. Both strands equally
Show answer

A. Lagging strand — Because DNA polymerase works only 5'-to-3', the lagging strand is made in short Okazaki fragments that are later joined by ligase; the leading strand is continuous.

Molecular Basis Of Inheritance

Q19. Because most amino acids are specified by more than one codon, the genetic code is described as:

  1. Degenerate
  2. Ambiguous
  3. Overlapping
  4. Non-universal
Show answer

A. Degenerate — Degeneracy means several codons can code for the same amino acid; the code is unambiguous (each codon specifies only one amino acid) and largely universal.

Evolution

Q20. The wings of a bird and the wings of an insect perform the same function but differ in structure and origin. They are best described as:

  1. Homologous organs showing divergent evolution
  2. Analogous organs showing convergent evolution
  3. Vestigial organs
  4. Homologous organs showing convergent evolution
Show answer

B. Analogous organs showing convergent evolution — Different structures adapted for a similar function are analogous and arise by convergent evolution, unlike homologous organs which share a common ancestral plan.

Evolution

Q21. According to the Hardy-Weinberg principle, allele frequencies in a population stay constant across generations provided that:

  1. No mutation, migration, selection or drift acts and mating is random
  2. Natural selection is strong
  3. Genetic drift occurs frequently
  4. Gene migration into the population is high
Show answer

A. No mutation, migration, selection or drift acts and mating is random — Genetic equilibrium requires no mutation, no gene migration, no selection, no genetic drift, and random mating; any of these forces changes allele frequencies (evolution).

Human Health And Disease

Q22. The transfer of antibodies from a mother to her foetus across the placenta gives the newborn:

  1. Active natural immunity
  2. Active artificial immunity
  3. Passive natural immunity
  4. Passive artificial immunity
Show answer

C. Passive natural immunity — Ready-made antibodies are received without the infant's own immune system responding, so it is passive; because it occurs naturally through the placenta, it is passive natural immunity.

Human Health And Disease

Q23. The stage of Plasmodium transmitted to a human through the bite of an infected female Anopheles mosquito is the:

  1. Merozoite
  2. Sporozoite
  3. Gametocyte
  4. Trophozoite
Show answer

B. Sporozoite — Infective sporozoites present in the mosquito's saliva enter the human bloodstream during the bite and then infect liver cells.

Microbes In Human Welfare

Q24. Biogas (gobar gas) obtained from cattle dung is produced mainly by the activity of:

  1. Methanogens
  2. Lactobacillus
  3. Rhizobium
  4. Nitrosomonas
Show answer

A. Methanogens — Methanogens (e.g., Methanobacterium) in anaerobic sludge break down organic matter and produce methane-rich biogas.

Biotechnology Principles

Q25. Restriction endonucleases cut DNA at palindromic recognition sites in a staggered fashion, generating:

  1. Blunt ends
  2. Single nucleotides
  3. RNA primers
  4. Sticky (cohesive) ends
Show answer

D. Sticky (cohesive) ends — Staggered cuts leave short single-stranded overhangs (sticky ends) whose complementary sequences allow foreign DNA to be joined into a vector.

Biotechnology Principles

Q26. The thermostable DNA polymerase used in PCR, which withstands the high denaturation temperature, is isolated from:

  1. Escherichia coli
  2. Bacillus thuringiensis
  3. Thermus aquaticus
  4. Agrobacterium tumefaciens
Show answer

C. Thermus aquaticus — Taq polymerase from the thermophilic bacterium Thermus aquaticus remains active at ~90 degrees C, so it need not be replenished after each denaturation step.
