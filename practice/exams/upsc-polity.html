Executive

Q1. The minimum age to be eligible for election as President of India is:

  1. 25 years
  2. 30 years
  3. 35 years
  4. 40 years
Show answer

C. 35 years — A person must have completed 35 years of age to be eligible for the office of President (Article 58). 25 is the minimum for the Lok Sabha, 30 for the Rajya Sabha.

Fundamental Rights

Q2. Which Article of the Constitution of India guarantees 'equality before the law'?

  1. Article 14
  2. Article 19
  3. Article 21
  4. Article 32
Show answer

A. Article 14 — Article 14 guarantees equality before the law and equal protection of the laws. Article 19 covers freedoms, Article 21 the right to life and liberty, and Article 32 the right to constitutional remedies.

Fundamental Rights

Q3. How many Fundamental Rights are currently guaranteed by the Constitution of India?

  1. 5
  2. 6
  3. 7
  4. 9
Show answer

B. 6 — There are currently 6 Fundamental Rights. Originally there were 7; the Right to Property was removed as a Fundamental Right by the 44th Amendment (1978) and made a legal right under Article 300A.

Parliament

Q4. The Rajya Sabha is also known as the:

  1. House of the People
  2. Council of States
  3. Lower House
  4. Legislative Assembly
Show answer

B. Council of States — The Rajya Sabha is the Council of States (the Upper House). The Lok Sabha is the House of the People (the Lower House).

Judiciary

Q5. The Supreme Court of India is located in:

  1. Mumbai
  2. Kolkata
  3. New Delhi
  4. Prayagraj
Show answer

C. New Delhi — The Supreme Court of India sits in New Delhi. (High Courts sit in various state capitals — for example, the Allahabad High Court is in Prayagraj.)

Parliament

Q6. The normal term of the Lok Sabha is:

  1. 4 years
  2. 5 years
  3. 6 years
  4. 7 years
Show answer

B. 5 years — The Lok Sabha's normal term is 5 years from the date of its first sitting, unless dissolved earlier. Rajya Sabha members serve 6-year terms and it is a permanent House.

Directive Principles

Q7. Which part of the Constitution of India deals with the Directive Principles of State Policy?

  1. Part III
  2. Part IV
  3. Part IVA
  4. Part V
Show answer

B. Part IV — Part IV (Articles 36 to 51) contains the Directive Principles of State Policy. Part III covers Fundamental Rights, and Part IVA (Article 51A) covers Fundamental Duties.

Constitutional Borrowings

Q8. The idea of incorporating a 'Preamble' in the Constitution of India was borrowed from the Constitution of which country?

  1. United Kingdom
  2. Ireland
  3. United States of America
  4. Canada
Show answer

C. United States of America — The concept of a Preamble was borrowed from the Constitution of the United States. Ireland gave India the Directive Principles, and Canada gave the model of a strong Centre.

Executive Ordinance

Q9. Under the Constitution, who has the power to promulgate an Ordinance when Parliament is not in session?

  1. The Prime Minister
  2. The President
  3. The Speaker of the Lok Sabha
  4. The Attorney General
Show answer

B. The President — Under Article 123, the President can promulgate ordinances when either House of Parliament is not in session. Such an ordinance has the same force as an Act of Parliament but must be laid before Parliament.

Judicial Doctrines

Q10. The 'Basic Structure' doctrine of the Constitution was propounded by the Supreme Court primarily in which case?

  1. Golaknath case (1967)
  2. Kesavananda Bharati case (1973)
  3. Minerva Mills case (1980)
  4. Maneka Gandhi case (1978)
Show answer

B. Kesavananda Bharati case (1973) — The Basic Structure doctrine was established in the Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala case (1973), which held that Parliament cannot amend the basic structure of the Constitution.

Writs

Q11. Which of the following writs is issued to a lower court or authority to prevent it from exceeding its jurisdiction?

  1. Mandamus
  2. Prohibition
  3. Quo Warranto
  4. Habeas Corpus
Show answer

B. Prohibition — The writ of Prohibition is issued by a higher court to a lower court or tribunal to stop it from proceeding beyond its jurisdiction. Mandamus commands performance of a duty, and Habeas Corpus deals with unlawful detention.

Centre State Relations

Q12. The Concurrent List of the Seventh Schedule, on which both the Union and the States can legislate, was adopted from the constitutional practice of which country?

  1. United States
  2. Australia
  3. Ireland
  4. South Africa
Show answer

B. Australia — The scheme of the Concurrent List was borrowed from the Constitution of Australia. In case of a conflict between Union and State law on a Concurrent subject, the Union law generally prevails.

Legislative Procedure

Q13. A Money Bill can be introduced in Parliament only in which House and only on the recommendation of whom?

  1. Rajya Sabha, on the recommendation of the President
  2. Lok Sabha, on the recommendation of the President
  3. Lok Sabha, on the recommendation of the Speaker
  4. Either House, on the recommendation of the Finance Minister
Show answer

B. Lok Sabha, on the recommendation of the President — Under Article 110, a Money Bill can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha and only on the recommendation of the President. The Rajya Sabha can only make recommendations, which the Lok Sabha may accept or reject.

Constitutional Bodies

Q14. Who among the following is the constitutional head of the Union Public Service Commission's counterpart at the State level and appoints the members of the State Public Service Commission?

  1. The Chief Minister
  2. The Governor
  3. The President
  4. The State Chief Justice
Show answer

B. The Governor — The Governor appoints the Chairman and members of a State Public Service Commission (Article 316), though they can be removed only by the President. UPSC members, by contrast, are appointed by the President.

Constitutional Amendments

Q15. The Fundamental Duties were added to the Constitution of India by which Amendment Act, based on the recommendations of the Swaran Singh Committee?

  1. 42nd Amendment Act, 1976
  2. 44th Amendment Act, 1978
  3. 24th Amendment Act, 1971
  4. 52nd Amendment Act, 1985
Show answer

A. 42nd Amendment Act, 1976 — The 42nd Amendment Act, 1976 added Fundamental Duties (Part IVA, Article 51A) on the recommendation of the Swaran Singh Committee. The 44th Amendment later added the duty to compensate; originally there were ten duties.

Emergency Provisions

Q16. Which article of the Constitution empowers the President to declare a National Emergency on grounds of war, external aggression, or armed rebellion?

  1. Article 352
  2. Article 356
  3. Article 360
  4. Article 365
Show answer

A. Article 352 — Article 352 provides for the proclamation of a National Emergency. Article 356 deals with President's Rule in states, and Article 360 deals with Financial Emergency.

Union Executive

Q17. Under the Constitution, the Vice-President of India is the ex-officio Chairman of which body?

  1. The Lok Sabha
  2. The Rajya Sabha
  3. The Planning Commission
  4. The National Development Council
Show answer

B. The Rajya Sabha — Under Article 64, the Vice-President is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha and presides over its sittings. The Speaker, who is elected from among its members, presides over the Lok Sabha.

Judiciary

Q18. The power of the Supreme Court to review its own judgment or order is provided for under which article?

  1. Article 32
  2. Article 137
  3. Article 143
  4. Article 226
Show answer

B. Article 137 — Article 137 grants the Supreme Court the power to review any judgment pronounced or order made by it. Article 143 deals with the advisory jurisdiction, and Article 32 is the right to constitutional remedies.

Schedules

Q19. The Seventh Schedule of the Constitution deals with which of the following?

  1. Allocation of seats in the Rajya Sabha
  2. Division of powers between the Union and States through three lists
  3. Languages recognized by the Constitution
  4. Forms of oaths and affirmations
Show answer

B. Division of powers between the Union and States through three lists — The Seventh Schedule contains the Union List, State List, and Concurrent List, dividing legislative powers between the Union and the States. The Fourth Schedule allocates Rajya Sabha seats, and the Eighth Schedule lists languages.

Parliament

Q20. Which constitutional provision requires that a person shall not be a member of both Houses of Parliament at the same time?

  1. It is permitted with the Speaker's consent
  2. The Constitution prohibits dual membership of Parliament
  3. It is allowed only for nominated members
  4. It is decided by the Election Commission case by case
Show answer

B. The Constitution prohibits dual membership of Parliament — Article 101 prohibits a person from being a member of both Houses simultaneously; if elected to both, the seat in one House must be vacated. This maintains the distinct composition of each House.

Anti Defection

Q21. Under the anti-defection law contained in the Tenth Schedule, who decides questions regarding the disqualification of a member on the ground of defection?

  1. The President or Governor
  2. The presiding officer of the House (Speaker or Chairman)
  3. The Election Commission
  4. The Supreme Court directly
Show answer

B. The presiding officer of the House (Speaker or Chairman) — Under the Tenth Schedule, the presiding officer (Speaker of the Lok Sabha or Chairman of the Rajya Sabha, and their counterparts in states) decides disqualification on grounds of defection, subject to judicial review.

Judiciary

Q22. The provision for a single, integrated judiciary with the Supreme Court at the apex and subordinate courts below it is a feature that reflects which principle?

  1. Dual polity with separate court systems
  2. A single integrated judicial system
  3. Complete separation of judiciary from executive with no appeals
  4. Judicial primacy over Parliament
Show answer

B. A single integrated judicial system — India has a single integrated judicial system in which the Supreme Court stands at the top, followed by High Courts and subordinate courts, enforcing both central and state laws. This differs from the dual court system of the United States.
